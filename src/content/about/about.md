# About Me

Hi, I'm Matthew.

Im I software engineer by trade, and I love building things. 

I'm a Senior Software Engineer with over a decade of experience building software across the stack. Most of my career has been spent building web applications with TypeScript, React, and Node.js, but what has kept me interested over the years hasn't been any particular framework—it's solving interesting problems.

I'm drawn to projects where there's a difficult technical challenge hiding beneath a simple user experience. Whether that's modernizing a legacy application, designing developer tooling, improving engineering workflows, or figuring out how to safely evolve a system that's been in production for years, I enjoy understanding how software works and finding thoughtful ways to make it better.

One of the things I enjoy most about engineering is taking something that feels overwhelming and turning it into a series of manageable problems. That usually means spending time understanding the existing system, asking a lot of "why?" questions, and resisting the urge to jump straight into writing code. I've found that the best technical decisions come from understanding the constraints as much as the technology.

Throughout my career I've had the opportunity to lead large frontend modernizations, build shared component libraries, improve CI/CD pipelines, work directly with clients, mentor other engineers, and more recently build AI-powered developer experiences. The technologies have changed over the years, but the parts of the job I enjoy most have stayed remarkably consistent: learning new systems, improving developer experience, and building software that people genuinely enjoy using.

Outside of work, I'm usually building something simply because I'm curious. I've spent time exploring desktop applications with Tauri, experimenting with Go, learning more about Linux and networking, and building an open-source presentation application inspired by ProPresenter. I enjoy projects that push me into unfamiliar territory because they're often where I learn the most.

I also enjoy writing about software engineering. Not because I think there is only one "right" way to build software, but because writing forces me to organize my thinking. My goal isn't to write framework tutorials that will be outdated in six months. Instead, I want to document the engineering decisions, tradeoffs, and lessons that come from working on real production systems. If something I've learned can help another engineer avoid a mistake or approach a problem differently, that's a worthwhile outcome.

When Im not working Im hanging out with my family going to museums, parks, or hiking. I take every chance to explore the world through my kids eyes.

### Working With Me

The teams I've enjoyed working with the most all share a few things in common. They value collaboration over ego, thoughtful engineering over unnecessary complexity, and understand that building good software is rarely the work of a single person. I enjoy partnering closely with product managers, designers, and other engineers to solve problems together, and I believe the best ideas usually come from open conversations rather than individual brilliance.

I like owning technical problems from start to finish. That might mean investigating an unfamiliar codebase, proposing an architectural direction, breaking a large initiative into manageable pieces, implementing the solution, and continuing to refine it as the product evolves. I enjoy mentoring engineers along the way and helping create environments where people feel comfortable asking questions, sharing ideas, and learning from each other.

At the end of the day, I simply enjoy building software. I like understanding systems, solving difficult problems, and leaving the code and the team in a better place than I found it.
